## The World Above

### Hex Crawling
Create an overland hex map using the circular loop system described on page 132 of the ShadowDark core rules. However, we recommend that each hex has a point of interest (instead of a 1-in-6 chance) and a 1-in-20 chance of a dungeon.
- **Points of Interest**. d20 tables to generate points of interest by terrain type. Roll separately for location and development to provide maximum variation. Results can include hazards and settlements (see below).
- **Terrain Hazards**. 2d6 tables to create environmental dangers. Beyond the points of interest tables, you can also assign hazards to any terrain as desired to raise the threat to the PCs.
- **Settlements**. d10 and d12 tables to generate settlements. Roll size randomly if desired or use the suggested size in the points of interest tables. These tables support 10 settlement sizes and 10 district types. d12 district tables produce even greater variation than the core rulebook.
- **Shops**. If the district table gives a shop result, you can roll it on these expanded d20 tables to produce greater variation and exciting new results.
- **Taverns**. If the district table gives a tavern result, you can roll it on these expanded d20 tables to produce greater variation and exciting new results. All new names, menu items, and drinks.
- **Farkle**. Rules for a dice gambling game to use as an alternative to Wizards and Thieves.
- **Acts of Devotion**. A religious alternative to carousing with excess wealth. Spread your faith to the community around you.
- **Combat Training**. A warrior's alternative to carousing with excess wealth. Learn new combat techniques in a tourney melee.
- **Magical Study**. An arcane alternative to carousing with excess wealth. Dive deep into arcane knowledge to expand your magical ability.

## Terrain Hazards
Hazards are a passive threat to adventurers. Unlike traps, you cannot avoid triggering them. They are often impossible to permanently destroy or disable. The fury of mother nature cannot be stopped by mere mortals. Choose the hazard's severity and use it to guide you on the strength of the challenge.
- Minor. Challenging for parties of average level 0-2.
- Normal. Challenging for parties of average level 3-6.
- Major. Challenging for parties of average level 7+.

**Movement**. Some hazards restrict or prevent movement. These hazards consume time and resources (like torches). Some hazards can even fully entrap characters. Without help, they will die when they run out of food and/or water.

**Damage**. Some hazards deal damage, either in a single hit or continuously every round. If this reduces a PC to zero HP, roll a death timer.

**Weakening**. Some hazards weaken, confuse, or impair the characters. For example, it might limit their senses in some way.

**Combining**. If you roll doubles (such as two 6s), combine all hazards on that row into a single extremely dangerous hazard.

## Farkle
Farkle is a two-player dice game that can be enjoyed in most taverns. The standard wager is 3 gp, but experienced gamblers may want to put more at stake. Be aware that the dice are fickle, and you can easily lose great sums in a moment of bad luck.  Each player rolls six dice and whoever has the highest total goes first. From that point on, players then alternate turns.  The active player rolls all six dice. They must then choose to score one or more dice (based on the criteria below).

Scoring:
- 1 is worth 10 points.
- 5 is worth 5 points.
- Three of a kind is worth 10 x the number shown. Three 3s would be worth 30 points.
- Four of a kind is worth double that value. Making four 3s worth 60 points (30 x 2).
- Five of a kind is worth double again. Making five 3s worth 120 points (60 x 2).
- If a player cannot score using any of the dice rolled (a bust), their turn ends. Play passes to the other player and no points are saved that round.

Decision Point:
- After scoring, the player must choose to either stay or press their luck.
- If they stay, the points scored on this turn are recorded and saved. If this gives them 250+ points, they win the game. If not, play passes to the other player.
- If they press their luck, they can collect the unscored dice and re-roll them. If all dice were just scored, they can make this re-roll with a full set of six dice.
- However, if the player busts while pressing their luck, all points rolled previously on that turn will be lost. The player must evaluate whether pressing their luck is a worthwhile strategy. The game can be won by slowly building up saved points or blitzing forward with a few high-scoring rolls.

### Dungeon Hazards
Hazards are a passive threat to adventurers. Unlike traps, you cannot avoid triggering them. They are often impossible to permanently destroy or disable. The fury of mother nature cannot be stopped by mere mortals.  Choose the hazard's severity and use it to guide you on the strength of the challenge.
- **Minor**. Challenging for parties of average level 0-2.
- **Normal**. Challenging for parties of average level 3-6.
- **Major**. Challenging for parties of average level 7+.

#### Hazards - Caves
**Movement.** Some hazards restrict or prevent movement. These hazards consume time and resources (like torches). Some hazards can even fully entrap characters. Without help, they will die when they run out of food and/or water.

**Damage.** Some hazards deal damage, either in a single hit or continuously every round. If this reduces a PC to zero HP, roll a death timer.

**Weakening.** Some hazards weaken, confuse, or impair the characters. For example, it might limit their senses in some way.

**Combining.** If you roll doubles (such as two 6s), combine all hazards on that row into a single extremely dangerous hazard.

## Monster Descriptions

#### Aarakocra
Gaunt, lightly feathered, winged humanoid with vulture-like beak and face. Long black talons on both hands and feet.
- **AC** 13 (natural), **HP** 9, **ATK** 2: bite / talon +2 (1d6), spear +3 (1d6), **MV** near (fly), **S** +2, **D** +2, **C** +0, **I** +0, **W** +0, **Ch** +0, **A**L C, **LV** 2
- **Carrion Feeder**. Consume a fifth of a helpless, unconscious, or dead humanoid within close distance. Regain 1d6 HP.
- **Carrion Tracker**. Can track dead flesh unerringly within a mile.
- **Dive**. Descend up to double near distance downward in straight line and make a talon attack. If hit, deals 3x damage.

#### Ant, Giant
Cattle-sized giant ant with ferocious snapping mandibles and long thin legs.
- **AC** 13 (dense chitin), **HP** 13, **ATK** 1: bite +4 (1d6 + grab), **MV** near, **S** +3, **D** +0, **C** +0, **I** +0, **W** +1, **Ch** +0, **AL** N, **LV** 3

## Treasure

### Glittering Prizes
These treasure tables are designed in the same style as the Treasure chapter of the ShadowDark core rulebook.  They provide an alternative, not a replacement for the core options.
- **Dungeon Dressing**. d100 yields a wide array of poor treasure, but also helpful in adding flavor to empty rooms in your dungeon.
- **Treasure 0-3**. d100 yields a mix of poor (45%), normal, (50%), and fabulous (5%) treasures for parties LV 0-3.
- **Treasure 4-6**. d100 yields a mix of poor (35%), normal, (50%), and fabulous (15%) treasures for parties LV 4-6.
- **Treasure 7-9**. d100 yields a mix of poor (35%), normal, (40%), fabulous (20%), and legendary treasures (5%) for parties LV 7-9.
- **Treasure 10+**. d100 yields a mix mixture of poor (15%), normal, (40%), fabulous (25%), and legendary treasures (20%) for parties LV 10+.
- **Boons**. d20s yield all new Oaths, Secrets, or Blessings to give out as non-physical treasure rewards.
- **Potions**. d20s to determine potion appearance, scent, and quirks + d20 to get a potion benefit and/or a d20 curse.
- **Magic Armor**. Standard tables to determine type and bonus for a random armor, supplemented with d20s to determine armor appearance, scent, and quirks + d20 to get a unique magic benefit and/or a d20 curse.
- **Magic Weapon**. Standard tables to determine type and bonus for a random weapon, supplemented with d20s to determine weapon appearance, scent, and quirks + d20 to get a unique weapon benefit and/or a d20 curse.
- **Magic Utility Item**. d100 yields a unique item type with d20s to determine item appearance, scent, and quirks + d20 to get a unique weapon benefit and/or a d20 curse.
- **Known Magic Items**. 85 new fully described magic items, including benefits, curses, and item personalities.
